# Vietnamese primary-school setting: historical review

Research date: 2026-10-01, Asia/Saigon. Read-only scene review; no Unreal assets,
story canon, engine settings or gameplay changed. No historical-authenticity
signoff is granted.

## Conclusion [HUMAN-NEEDED]

The scene is partly plausible for a Vietnamese primary school with furniture
retained from around 2000-2002. Its basic classroom equipment is well chosen.
The present visual treatment does not yet make the location convincingly a
particular Vietnamese school from that period. The main weaknesses are regional
architectural context, culturally specific classroom surfaces, material identity
and the legibility of the school's passage through time. These are art-direction
judgments, not demonstrated nationwide historical violations.

No clearly post-2002 device was identified in the inspected views. That is not
a complete anachronism clearance: several surfaces are blank or too coarse to
identify, and the exterior, book editions and character clothing are unassessed.

## What the comparison is actually about

V3 section 4 places the present in **2026**, in an isolated reservoir valley.
Section 5 says the school was the evacuation assembly point and that its desks
remain arranged as in **2002**. Thus the target is an older school encountered
in 2026, with a specific preserved 2002 arrangement. It is not automatically a
school operating normally in 2000, nor a building entirely frozen in 2002.

The inspected canon passages do not establish a real province, a construction
year, a regional building prototype, continuous abandonment or a maintenance
history. Central-Vietnam references below are candidates, not a decision that
Khe Lac is in Quang Tri or Quang Nam. Do not add that geography to canon.
Likewise, do not redesign the desk arrangement without documenting how it
remains consistent with section 5.

## Historical reference register

**R1 - Period primary source, 2000.** Ministry of Education and Training,
Decision22/2000, historical articles44-45, on the official legal database.
Article45 describes age-appropriate student seating, a teacher's desk/chair,
writing board, teaching platform and lights/fans where grid electricity exists.
Article44 recognizes regional designs and a campus with learning, administrative,
play and service spaces. This describes period expectations, not universal
compliance and not present-day design advice. [Official text](https://vbpl.moj.gov.vn/bogiaoducdaotao/Pages/vbpq-toanvan.aspx?ItemID=5842&Keyword=).

**R2 - Builder's dated project register, 2000-2002.** VNAH/HEV lists Hiep Duc
primary school in Quang Nam under2000, Nhu Quynh A in Hung Yen under2001 as a
two-story ten-classroom building, and Tan Hop in Quang Tri and a flood-area
school in An Giang under2002. The accompanying descriptions vary considerably.
These project-year labels establish diversity; they do not independently date
each photograph or prescribe Khe Lac's architecture. [Project register](https://www.vnah-hev.org/projects/schools--for-rural--ethnic-minority-children-in-vietnam/).

**R3 - Explicitly dated archival photograph metadata, 1999.** UNICEF identifies
`UNI32805/LeMoyne` as a primary-school classroom at Ngoc Lu, Ha Nam, in1999.
The retrospective article is dated2025; the photograph's stated date is1999.
This is a useful reference to inspect later, not visual evidence used here for
wall decoration or furniture details. [UNICEF archive essay](https://www.unicef.org/vietnam/stories/legacy-partnership-every-child).

**R4 - Period-adjacent first-hand reporting, 2008.** Bao Dong Nai describes
rural-primary-school desks, older wooden legs and newer metal legs, including
real problems matching furniture to young children's size. Its statement about
supply since2000 is local journalistic reporting, not proof that every school
used the same design. Both retained wood furniture and early-2000s metal frames
are plausible; wood alone cannot date a room. [Dong Nai report](https://baodongnai.com.vn/xahoi/giaoduc/200810/dung-hoc-2048339/).

**R5 - Retrospective first-person account, 2017.** SGGP recalls cassette use
in early-2000s student life, language listening and radio-journalism coursework.
This supports the technology's period plausibility, not installation of the
scene's exact player in a primary-school corridor. [SGGP account](https://www.sggp.org.vn/cassette-thoi-xa-vang-post461297.html).

**R6 - Standards provenance only.** VSQI identifies TCVN3978:1984 as a general
school design standard. The full standard was not read or purchased. No numeric
dimension or mandatory open-corridor claim is attributed to it. Do not use
TCVN8793:2011 as if it were evidence of what existed in2002. [VSQI catalogue](https://tieuchuan.vsqi.gov.vn/tieuchuan/view?sohieu=TCVN+3978%3A1984).

**R7 - School's own historical account.** Khuong Mai primary school describes
its1999 opening with three three-story blocks. This urban counterexample helps
avoid equating all Vietnamese schools around2000 with a poor one-story school.
[School history](https://thkhuongmai.hanoi.edu.vn/giao-duc-truyen-thong/to-chuc-tham-quan-phong-truyen-thong-nha-truong/ctmb/20665/565).

## Findings [HUMAN-NEEDED]

### 1. Student desks and benches: retain, refine rather than replace

The desk preview has a planked wooden top, shelf and separate shared bench.
The classroom image shows repeated rows oriented toward the teaching end.
This is a plausible retained furniture vocabulary; R4 supports wood furniture
and warns against assuming only one nationwide pattern. The current render's
strong swirled wood and repetitive damage reduce material credibility. Add
specific joints, repair history and localized wear when an art pass is authorized.
No conclusion is made about ergonomic fit without the intended pupil age and
an appropriate dimensional reference. Bench seating is not inherently wrong.

### 2. Blackboard, teacher furniture and platform: historically supported

A dark writing board and raised teaching end are visible. R1 explicitly supports
this equipment vocabulary in2000. A platform is not evidence that the scene is
too old for2002. The board lacks legible lesson remnants in this inspected view;
that weakens school identity but is not proof the equipment type is wrong.
Preserve space and access needed by the clue interactions.

### 3. Lights and ceiling fan: plausible, exact model unqualified

The scene includes tube-like fittings and a ceiling fan. R1 supports electric
lights/fans in supplied schools. That does not authenticate this fitting's exact
age, a specific fan blade count, color temperature or brightness. There is no
basis to demand three blades, ban all four-blade fans, or assume every rural
school lacked electricity. Current lighting exposes material repetition and
obscures some surfaces; atmospheric preference is not chronology evidence.

### 4. Architecture: highest-impact identity weakness, not proven impossibility

The inspected hall reads as a sealed institutional interior with continuous
walls, a dominant ceiling element, repeat doors and little connection to a yard.
It is a weak match to the proposed remote-valley identity. Sources R1, R2 and R7
show regional and funding differences; they do not prove enclosed corridors were
absent from Vietnam. An open veranda, visible yard and simpler structural rhythm
are promising reference directions, contingent on a selected real school type.
Do not rebuild the gameplay route merely to satisfy a stereotype.

The blue-gray overhead object must not be declared a modern HVAC duct from the
image alone: the source kit has a concrete corridor-beam component. The visual
issue is that its construction/material reading is ambiguous. Historic dimensions
and the full exterior cannot be qualified from these captures.

### 5. Windows, roof and campus: insufficient coverage

Source intent includes wood frames, shutters and two classroom windows. The
front-facing classroom capture does not show them well enough for detailed
regional comparison. There is no reviewed roof or campus elevation. A flat
interior ceiling can exist beneath a sloping metal roof; the caption about rain
on metal roofing is not, by itself, a demonstrated architectural contradiction.
Gate, yard, planting, drainage and roof should remain unassessed, not marked absent.

### 6. Classroom identity: too generic in inspected views

The front board is largely blank, a side frame is pale/unidentifiable, and there
is little readable classroom information in the visible area. The HUD labels
the school, but a HUD cannot establish environmental authenticity. Candidate
references include class identification, timetable, handwriting/lesson remains,
wall maps and an age-appropriate school emblem/display arrangement. Portraits,
the Five Teachings or a specific slogan should be sourced to a period/local
reference, rather than claimed universally mandatory from R1: that text did not
establish their exact arrangement. The whole map has not been proven to lack
these objects. Source books and clue displays need separate close-up review.

### 7. Floors and wall finish: compatible palette, weak physical specificity

Muted painted plaster and a lower wall band are not identifiable anachronisms.
The floor looks broadly continuous and swirled in the inspected hall, making its
construction hard to recognize. This is a material-readability issue. Cement,
terrazzo or tiles are possible directions to compare with a chosen school;
No single finish is prescribed as a nationwide period requirement.
Painting everything yellow or uniformly weathering every surface would not
resolve regional authenticity. Repairs and water damage need different causes
and locations, not one procedural noise layer across all props.

### 8. Cassette and loudspeaker: retain technology, improve identifiability

The cassette player is consistent with the story's recordings, and R5 supports
early-2000s use of the medium. Its pale block shape and barely distinguishable
controls do not identify a real product family. Refine the tape door, controls,
speaker/vent details, power and plausible connection to the broadcast setup
against a dated reference. R5 does not justify asserting that this exact deck
or mounting was standard primary-school equipment. Keep the new shelf unless a
functional/reference reason warrants changing it; it is reversible dressing.

### 9. Preserved2002 arrangement seen in2026: aging story still unresolved

V3 mandates retained desk arrangement, not universal decay or uninterrupted
abandonment. The renders do not explain which surfaces were repaired, which
objects survived the flood, or whether the school has been used since then.
Choose a reversible maintenance-history art assumption before distributing
stains, new fittings or preserved paperwork. Do not invent a flood-water level,
school-closure year or documented repair event as canon.

### 10. Paper, curriculum and characters: no clearance

The close-ups do not establish textbook editions, dated forms, notebook printing,
uniforms or the human figures' period clothing. These are high-value chronology
checks once legible assets exist. In a2002 document/echo, avoid copying a modern
school form or recent textbook cover by habit. In the2026 present, a later object
is not automatically an anachronism. No new named pupil or historical testimony
is proposed by this review.
